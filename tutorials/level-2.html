The Persian Alphabet

The Persian alphabet consists of 32 letters, extending the Arabic alphabet with four additional characters: \u067E (pe), \u0686 (che), \u0698 (zhe), and \u06AF (gaf). Each letter has up to four forms depending on its position in a word: isolated, initial, medial, and final.

Letter Groups

Persian letters are traditionally organized into groups that share a similar base shape. Learning one letter in a group gives you the foundation for all others in that group \u2014 they differ only by dots.

The Ba Group: \u0628 \u067E \u062A \u062B

These letters share the same basic form \u2014 a shallow, horizontal boat shape. The only difference between them is the number and placement of dots: ba (\u0628) has one dot below, pe (\u067E) has three dots below, ta (\u062A) has two dots above, and se (\u062B) has three dots above.

The Ba Group \u2014 Same base shape, different dots

The Jim Group: \u062C \u0686 \u062D \u062E

These letters share a rounded, bowl-like form. Jim (\u062C) has one dot below, che (\u0686) has three dots below, he-jimi (\u062D) has no dots, and khe (\u062E) has one dot above. The bowl shape in Nastaliq is deep and elegant, curving below the baseline.

The Dal Group: \u062F \u0630

Dal (\u062F) and zal (\u0630) are among the simplest letters \u2014 a small angular stroke with a hook. Zal is simply dal with a dot above. These are non-connecting letters: they connect to the letter before them but not after.

The Re Group: \u0631 \u0632 \u0698

Re (\u0631), ze (\u0632), and zhe (\u0698) share a simple curved tail form. Like the dal group, these are non-connecting letters. The curve drops below the baseline gracefully \u2014 in Nastaliq, this tail is one of the most characteristic features.

The Sin Group: \u0633 \u0634

Sin (\u0633) and shin (\u0634) feature three small teeth followed by a sweeping bowl. In Nastaliq, the teeth are often simplified into a smooth wave. These are among the wider letters and demand careful spacing.

Letter Anatomy

Each letter has specific anatomical features that must be understood before writing:

  • Sareh (head): The top of the letter, where the stroke typically begins.
  • Taneh (body): The main form of the letter.
  • Dombaaleh (tail): The descending stroke below the baseline.
  • Kashideh (extension): The horizontal connecting stroke between letters.
  • Nuqta (dot): The distinguishing marks above or below.

Key Concept

In Nastaliq, letters are written on a descending diagonal \u2014 each letter sits slightly lower than the one before it. This "hanging" quality (the literal meaning of "Nastaliq") gives the script its distinctive cascading rhythm.

Practice Method

Write each letter group in all four positions before moving to the next group. Fill entire pages with a single letter. The traditional mashq (practice) method demands repetition until the hand moves without conscious thought.

Exercises

  1. Group practice: Write each letter group (ba, jim, dal, re, sin) in isolation, one full page per group.
  2. Four-form drill: For each connecting letter, write it in all four positions across a line.
  3. Proportion check: Write the letter alef 20 times and measure each with your nuqta. Are they all the same height?
  4. Dot placement: Write ba, pe, ta, se in sequence. Ensure dots are consistently placed.
  5. Mirror pairs: Write dal/zal, re/ze, sin/shin side by side. The base forms should be identical.
