The Scripts Compared

The same phrase rendered in three different scripts reveals each script's distinct character.

\u062F\u0631 \u0646\u0627\u0645 \u062E\u062F\u0627\u0648\u0646\u062F

Naskh

Upright, structured, measured. Each letter sits clearly on the horizontal baseline. The script of precision.

\u062F\u0631 \u0646\u0627\u0645 \u062E\u062F\u0627\u0648\u0646\u062F

Nastaliq

Flowing, diagonal, cascading. Words hang from the right and descend to the left. The script of poetry.

\u062F\u0631 \u0646\u0627\u0645 \u062E\u062F\u0627\u0648\u0646\u062F

Shekasteh

Rapid, broken, expressive. Letters merge and overlap. Dots vanish. The script of personal voice.

Individual Letters

The beauty of Persian calligraphy exists even at the level of a single character.

\u0627

Alef

The vertical stroke. Simplicity itself \u2014 and therefore the most demanding letter to write perfectly.

\u0639

Eyn

The most complex letter in the alphabet. Its forms change dramatically across the four positions.

\u0646

Nun

The bowl and tail. Nun's descending curve is one of Nastaliq's most characteristic gestures.

\u0647

He

Four entirely different forms depending on position. The chameleon of the Persian alphabet.
